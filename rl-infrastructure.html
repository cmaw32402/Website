Multi-Robot Reinforcement Learning and Sim-to-Real Infrastructure

Ongoing Research · SMART Lab / Master's Thesis

This project develops a simulation-to-real research framework for multi-robot formation control and flocking using Hamster S robots. The work combines physical robot modeling, vision-based localization, and reinforcement learning infrastructure to investigate how robot teams can maintain coordination and recover from disruptions.

Simulation environment

A Webots simulation environment supports repeatable multi-robot experiments with randomized initial configurations, navigation targets, and varying team sizes. The environment provides a controlled setting for developing and evaluating coordination policies, including scenarios involving changes in team composition and robot behavior.

Robot models and simulation infrastructure

Custom Hamster S models were developed in Webots using measured physical dimensions and wheel geometry. The models reproduce the robots' differential-drive configuration and support controlled evaluation of coordination policies in simulation prior to physical deployment.

Learning-based formation control

The reinforcement learning infrastructure supports episodic training with configurable reward functions and interchangeable network architectures, allowing different learning approaches to be tested within the same simulation framework.

The research focuses on graph-based multi-agent policies for maintaining formations and coordinated flocking under disruptions, including robot failures or impaired mobility, robots joining or leaving the team, and unexpected changes in position. The goal is to learn policies that can adapt to these changes and restore coordinated behavior rather than relying on fixed team configurations.

Localization and physical integration

Physical robot localization uses overhead camera tracking and ArUco markers to estimate robot poses. Camera calibration and lens undistortion address optical distortion, while homography-based pixel-to-world mapping transforms image detections into arena coordinates and accounts for perspective effects. Improving robustness to noisy detections remains an ongoing focus.

This infrastructure supports the eventual deployment of simulated controllers on physical Hamster S robots and comparison of their behavior across environments.

Evaluation plan

Planned evaluations compare learned policies against classical formation controllers, focusing on formation accuracy, collision avoidance, scalability across team sizes, and recovery following disruptions. Physical experiments will also be used to examine differences between simulated and real robot behavior. The research is ongoing, and quantitative results are not yet presented.
